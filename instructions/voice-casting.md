# Choose and change story voices

Read the entire story before choosing its narrator. Reuse the user's selected provider, language, accent, narrator identity and saved audition when suitable. An unset/delegated voice is the agent's casting task inside the actual tool, privacy and spending scope. Do not ask a routine gender/voice questionnaire or force a paid provider. A request for excellent performance is a quality target; it does not establish that a voice is perfect or that listening occurred.

## Story-specific casting

1. Write a short performance brief in `audio/voice-plan.json` with the current full-story/source hash. For `NARRATOR`, identify narrative viewpoint/distance, genre, audience, emotional trajectory, clarity, warmth/gravity, restraint, natural pacing, accent/language and pronunciation needs. Account for intimate scenes, exposition, suspense, humor and the ending where actually present. Avoid a generic dramatic/trailer voice for every film. Distinguish the narrator from character dialogue. A silent film has no voice-casting work.
2. For each speaking `CHAR-###`, derive a distinct voice identity from the written role, age/design, personality, relationships and required emotional range. Record which aspects the author specified and which are proposed. Do not infer an accent from ethnicity or replace a stated preference with a stereotype. Preserve identity across all parts/full cut; use supported delivery directions for different emotions rather than changing the actor on every line.
3. Check actual provider/model/language/voice availability, controls, usage evidence, runtime/account and costs. Shortlist only suitable available voices within the selected scope. Reuse a suitable current audition; when selection is uncertain, compare a small shortlist (usually two or three) on the same representative story passage, including important names and the difficult emotional/dialogue beat. Do not generate a full film's speech to choose its narrator or call every provider. Any sample upload/expense needs the existing applicable scope.
4. Generate actual samples with `studio speech --purpose audition`, using `--speaker NARRATOR` or the character ID. Listen to the actual files with an available playback tool. Assess intelligibility, pronunciation, natural rhythm, emotional fit without overacting, stability and listener fatigue. Compare the required emotional range, not only a pleasant isolated sentence. Record real sample paths/hashes, listening notes, weaknesses and the chosen reason; do not fabricate scores, auditions or a listening pass. If playback or a necessary model/voice is unavailable, report the concrete limitation and keep the choice provisional.
5. Select the best fit among the evaluated options with `studio voice set`. Put the actual selection and rationale in the plan, reference the audition evidence and keep the effective provider/model/voice in `audio/narrator.json`. The command records a decision/history and affected parts; it does not listen or generate speech. Complete pronunciation and restrained per-turn delivery notes. Fast casting inside actual delegated scope is agent work; Strict still follows its real G5 decision.

The plan's `roles` object uses stable speaker IDs. Each entry should contain `performance_brief`, `candidates` (actual provider/model/voice IDs, suitability reasons and real audition evidence), `selected`, `selection_reason` and `listening_status`. An audition entry identifies its file, actual SHA-256, listener/tool, notes and verdict. Set `status` to provisional or selected according to the actual evidence; the template begins draft. The plan supports judgment and does not invent a numeric ranking engine. Keep this story's cast out of reusable global presets.

Fast capability fallback: if this session cannot hear audio, report that once and retain provisional casting rather than inventing listening notes. Do not add a routine separate audition approval loop. Use the rights-verified draft mix and ask for actual voice/sound/caption acceptance in the one complete-preview decision, highlighting genuinely uncertain words. Only after the real human listened may `approve-preview --listening-reviewed` record that evidence in `production/preview-listening.json` and link it from the voice plan. Its scope is the actual selected performance and used sound ranges, not unused source clips. Strict still requires its real G5 evidence. A genuinely unresolved major casting choice or a provider/budget change still needs that meaningful decision.

## Changes at any stage

Users can ask to change the narrator or any character during planning, after a preview, or after a delivery. Interpret natural requests such as “make the narrator warmer” as a request to choose/audition a suitable replacement, not as a reason to restart intake. Keep the provider and all other configuration unless the requested change actually requires another route. Preserve an explicitly selected voice until the user asks to change it; delegated refinement stays inside scope. Do not ask for the same permission again after the user has requested the concrete change.

Use `studio voice set` with the actual change quotation and source reference when replacing a bound choice. The scoped receipt updates only the voice choice in an existing current Fast authorization, preserving its original quotation/configuration in history. It cannot repair a stale unrelated configuration or expand tools, upload/privacy permissions or budget. A new provider or expense outside scope requires only that material scope decision. Existing delegated story-edit permission remains valid after a voice-only amendment. Strict changes stale the affected G5/G6 records and require the applicable renewed decision.

Old profiles are retained exactly in `audio/voice-revisions/` and change evidence in `audio/voice-decisions.json`; an earlier voice can be chosen again from that history. Changing NARRATOR does not replace the character cast. Apply a new identity consistently across its relevant parts; a deliberate within-story voice change needs a story reason and explicit mappings. Presets save the actual current narrator, including a narrator role override, without copying the previous story's character voices.

Regenerate the changed speaker's clips and reuse unaffected clip caches. Rebuild the affected part's measured alignment/timeline, mix/subtitles and preview/master; inspect camera/reveal timing when speech duration changes. Reuse unchanged source images. Reassemble an included full cut from the current parts, inspect transitions and perform actual playback. Show the updated preview under the existing workflow, since the performance changed. Preserve old deliveries and export the replacement as a new revision. Never claim an instantaneous, free or fully verified voice replacement before this work occurs.
