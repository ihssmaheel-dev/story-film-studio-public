# Story-driven image planning

Choose the image count from the complete story, its audience, genre, emotional rhythm and actual performance. The agent makes this decision inside the configured scope. Do not ask the user to supply an image count or approve each shot unless they requested that control. There is no images-per-minute quota, default 6–8-image preset, equal-duration grid or requirement to use every authorized image call.

## Decide what the viewer needs to see

1. Retain the complete source read, scene map, character/state dependencies and ending. Identify the opening question, meaningful actions/decisions, emotional turns, setups, reveals and resolution. For a part, use the whole-story context and its actual continuation/payoff too.
2. Give every image a specific narrative purpose. Open on a readable, interesting story moment. Add a shot when a change of location/time, physical state, focal detail, information or emotion needs another view. Give important reactions and the ending enough room to land. Vary wide/medium/close framing when it serves orientation, intimacy or discovery; avoid arbitrary variety.
3. Combine adjacent beats when one coherent image sustains them. Hold a reaction or shared dialogue frame when its meaning develops through the performance. Do not cut on every sentence or speaker change, pad with unrelated scenery, repeat a near-identical image merely to reset attention, or force incompatible actions/poses into a single still. A critical action/state change must remain visible even if that needs more images.
4. Protect causality, identity, geography and reveal order. A later-state image cannot appear while narration is still describing an earlier state. Establish necessary geography before confusing coverage. Keep clues legible at their intended moment without revealing the twist early. Leave safe room for the actual subtitles.
5. Choose the smallest set that clearly carries those beats and holds attention. Fewer images are useful only while the story remains understandable and engaging. Action, suspense, quiet drama and dialogue can require different counts at the same runtime. Explain the count from the story, rather than claiming a numeric count guarantees engagement.

## Record once, then check actual timing

Keep `image_planning` inside the selected part's `shots.json`; use [the structural example](../templates/image-planning.example.json). Bind it to the actual `creative/story-revised.md` hash when present, otherwise `creative/original.md`. `rationale` briefly explains the overall count, rhythm and intentional holds. Its ordered `shots` list contains each written `shot_id` once, a `purpose` describing what the viewer gains and an `entry_reason` explaining why that view begins there. The first entry describes the opening; later entries explain the change from the previous shot. This is concise agent reasoning, not an approval or proof of artistic quality. Keep full visual descriptions and exact spoken words in the ordinary shot records; do not duplicate them here.

Author cuts with measured `speech_turns` anchors where suitable. Provisional timing is only a planning assumption. After actual speech/alignment, inspect the real hold lengths, dialogue/reaction transitions, silent action, suspense and ending. `studio next` carries current timing context and image/reference counts. Review both a hold that drags and a cut that rushes in context; there is no universal minimum/maximum hold. Inspect the actual full preview for continuity, readability and engagement. Do not claim this happened from a successful validator.

Merge redundant shots or split a missing beat only when that inspection warrants it. Reuse unchanged verified images; local pans/zooms need a story reason and do not supply missing action. Rebuild affected timing/presentation and show the changed preview under the existing workflow. Do not regenerate everything because the voice changed or because a planned duration differed from measured speech.

Count final story images, shared style/cast/location/prop references, reused files and repair calls separately. A shared reference can serve multiple roles; generate only references needed for continuity. The configured call/budget cap is a ceiling, never a target. If the necessary plan exceeds it, present the concrete scope choice without silently cutting essential story beats or exceeding the cap. Existing plans without `image_planning` remain usable; include the record when authoring a new plan or substantively replanning one, without migrating old deliveries merely for metadata.
